A media kit isn't a portfolio. Nobody's reading it end to end — a brand marketer opens it, scans for three or four numbers, and decides whether to keep reading your pitch. Optimize for the skim, not for completeness.

## What actually needs to be on it

- **Name, tagline, niche, location.** One line each. The tagline is the thing that tells a brand what you make in five words — "tech reviews for indie hackers," not "content creator and storyteller."
- **Platform stats — with engagement, not just followers.** Follower count alone is a vanity metric brands are increasingly discounting. A 40K-follower account with 6% engagement outperforms a 200K account with 0.8%. List both, per platform.
- **Total reach.** One number that sums your audience across every platform, at the top. It's the first thing a brand's eyes go to.
- **A short bio.** Two or three sentences on what you make and who's watching. Not your life story — brands are checking fit, fast.
- **Past brand collabs.** Even two or three is social proof. "Sponsored video, 40K views" tells a brand you've done this before and know what a deliverable looks like.
- **Rates, or a clear note that they're available on request.** Leaving this blank with no note reads as unfinished. Say something, even if it's just "rates available on request."
- **Contact info.** Obvious, but it's the single most common omission — a beautiful media kit with no email is a dead end.

## What gets a media kit ignored

**Stale numbers.** A media kit with a follower count from six months ago undersells you, or worse, gets caught in a discrepancy when the brand checks your profile directly. Update it before every pitch round, not once a year.

**Vague niche.** "Lifestyle" tells a brand nothing. "Budget home cooking for people who hate meal prep" tells them exactly whether their product fits.

**No ask.** A media kit that doesn't end with what you want — a paid post, a gifted collab, an intro call — leaves the brand to guess your next step. Say it.

**Length.** One page. Two if you have a lot of platforms. A media kit brands have to scroll through multiple times is a media kit that gets closed.
