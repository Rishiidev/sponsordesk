Getting paid to post is one deal. Getting paid for a brand to *keep using* what you posted is a separate one — and it's the one most creators quietly give away for free.

## What usage rights actually cover

When you post a sponsored piece of content, you own it by default. A brand paying you for the post buys the right to have you post it — not necessarily the right to do anything else with it. Three separate things usually get bundled into "usage rights," and they're worth pricing separately:

- **Organic usage on your own account.** What you agreed to when you took the deal — the post lives on your feed. This is the base rate.
- **Extended usage** — the brand reposting your content on *their own* social accounts, website, or email. This goes beyond your feed and is worth more than the base rate, because it extends your reach into the brand's audience too.
- **Whitelisting / paid usage** — the brand running your content as a paid ad, through their own ad account, often targeted beyond either of your audiences entirely. This is the most valuable use of all, because it's directly tied to ad performance the brand is measuring and optimizing against. It should cost the most, and often does — usage-rights riders on whitelisted content can meaningfully exceed the base rate for the post itself.

## Why this costs creators money

Two ways, usually:

**It's underpriced because it's undernamed.** If a contract just says "usage rights included" with no scope, no channel list, and no end date, you've likely granted broad usage for free — because you didn't know to ask what "included" meant. Vague usage-rights language almost always favors whoever wrote the contract.

**It doesn't expire when you think it does.** This is the one that actually costs money after the fact: a brand licenses your content for, say, 90 days of paid whitelisting — then keeps the ad running past day 90 because nobody on either side is tracking the window. The brand isn't necessarily acting in bad faith; usage-rights expiration just isn't something most contract-tracking (a shared drive, a Notion doc, an inbox) is built to flag. The content keeps earning the brand ad performance. You stop getting paid for it the day the license should have ended.

## What to put in the contract

- **An explicit end date**, not "ongoing" or left blank.
- **Explicit channels** — organic only, or organic + brand's owned channels, or + paid whitelisting. Name them.
- **A renewal clause that means a new payment**, not an automatic extension. If a brand wants to keep running the ad past the window, that's a new negotiation, not a free extension.
