Brands don't have an AP department waiting for your invoice the way a freelance client's finance team might. The marketing manager who booked the deal usually has to forward it to someone else to get you paid — which means a vague or incomplete invoice doesn't just get paid slower, it gets *lost*.

Here's what actually needs to be on it, and when to send it.

## What to include

- **Invoice number.** Doesn't need to be fancy — `INV-0001` is fine — but it needs to exist. It's the reference the brand's finance team uses to match your invoice to their PO or approval. No number, no easy match, more likely it sits in someone's inbox.
- **What you delivered, described the way the contract described it.** If the contract says "1 Instagram feed post + 2 stories," your invoice should say exactly that — not "sponsored content." Mismatched wording is one of the most common reasons an invoice bounces back for "clarification."
- **The date the deliverable went live**, not the date you're invoicing. Brands often pay on a clock that starts from posting, not from signing.
- **Payment terms.** Net 15, Net 30 — whatever you agreed to. If you didn't explicitly agree to terms, default to Net 15 and say so on the invoice itself, so it's not ambiguous after the fact.
- **How to pay you.** Bank details or a payment link. Don't make them ask.
- **Your business info**, even if "your business" is just you. Name, email, and a mailing address if you're in the US and might need to send a W-9.

## When to send it

Immediately after the deliverable goes live — not before. Brands are (reasonably) wary of paying for a post that hasn't posted yet, and invoicing early is a common reason payment gets held pending "confirmation of delivery." Post first, screenshot it, invoice same day.

## The follow-up problem

This is where most solo creators actually lose money — not from brands refusing to pay, but from an invoice quietly going stale in someone's inbox while you're busy with the next deal. A brand goes quiet, three weeks pass, and chasing a two-month-old invoice feels awkward enough that people just... don't.

Treat unpaid invoices the same way you'd treat an unanswered pitch: a follow-up at day 7, another at day 14 if needed. Net-15 terms with a reminder that actually fires on day 16 recovers more revenue than a strongly-worded contract ever will.
