Search "creator CRM" and you'll land on GRIN, CreatorIQ, or July. Sign up for a trial and it takes about ten minutes to realize none of them were built with you in mind.

That's not a knock on any of them — they're solving a different problem for a different buyer. Here's the actual difference, so you stop wasting a trial period figuring it out yourself.

## Who each one is actually for

| | Built for | Minimum spend | Who's the user |
|---|---|---|---|
| **GRIN** | Brands running influencer programs | Custom quote — no public pricing, third-party research puts entry tiers around $400/mo and up, enterprise deals into five figures a year | A marketing team managing hundreds of creators |
| **July** | Agencies managing creator rosters | $50/mo minimum, plus a per-creator add-on, billed quarterly | An agency manager running deals for multiple clients |
| **SponsorDesk** | A single creator running their own deals | Free up to 3 active deals, $9/mo founder pricing beyond that | The creator themselves |

The pattern: GRIN and July both price and design around *managing other people*. GRIN's buyer is a brand marketer sourcing creators for a campaign. July's buyer is an agency account manager running a roster. Both tools are good at that job.

Neither one is priced, or built, for a solo creator with 200K subscribers who's closing their own six deals a month and doesn't have a team.

## Where this actually shows up

It's not just pricing. The gap shows up in what the product assumes about you:

**Campaign vs. relationship.** GRIN is organized around campaigns — a brand recruits a batch of creators, tracks deliverables across all of them, measures ROI at the campaign level. You're not running campaigns. You're running one relationship with one brand at a time, and the next one looks nothing like the last.

**Roster vs. self.** July is organized around a roster — one agency dashboard, many creators underneath it, each with their own deals. If you're managing yourself, you're paying for a multi-seat structure you'll never use, and configuring around an org chart of one.

**Team pricing vs. solo pricing.** Both tools' pricing scales with the number of people or creators *you* manage. You manage exactly one creator: you. That's the whole reason a $9/mo flat plan looks strange next to a $400+/mo custom quote — they're not the same product shaped differently, they're solving adjacent-but-different problems.

## What actually matters for a solo creator

If you're the one signing the deals, the job isn't "manage a roster" — it's:

- Not losing a brand in your inbox between "interested" and "signed"
- Not missing a follow-up window (the biggest source of lost revenue on a solo pipeline — a brand goes quiet, you get busy, three weeks pass and the deal's dead)
- Knowing what you charged the last three brands so you're not making up a number on every call
- Not blowing past a usage-rights window because nobody's tracking it but you
- Getting paid without chasing an invoice manually

None of that needs a campaign dashboard or a multi-seat roster. It needs a pipeline, reminders, and a paper trail — sized for one person.

## The honest recommendation

- **Running influencer campaigns for a brand?** Use GRIN or CreatorIQ. That's exactly what they're for.
- **Running deals across a roster of creators for clients?** Use July. Same logic.
